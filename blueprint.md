# The Open Ecosystem Success Story

**Blueprint v1.1** · Open Ecosystem Collective · CC BY-SA 4.0

---

## What it is

A written account of a real project, authored by every company that built it, published only if all of them agree, and carried by all of them at once.

A conventional success story names the companies with a marketing budget. This one names everyone who did the work, at equal weight.

## The one rule

**All or nothing.** The Story publishes only if every party agrees, to the Story and to every channel. One no ends it everywhere.

The rule is a property of the format, never a personal demand from the author. Everything else is self-accountability, and nothing else is enforced.

## When a project qualifies

- Several independent companies delivered one thing for one customer.
- The customer is willing to take part and to open the door to the partners.
- There's enough written residue to reconstruct who did what.
- The hard parts can stay in.

## Roles

**The author** writes the base story and keeps the whole neutral. If the author is connected to one of the parties, that party gets the same weight as every other, and the brief says so.

**The customer** sees the base story first, approves the project for the format and introduces the author to every partner. After that, one party among the others.

**Each party** writes its own section and quote, and approves the Story and every channel. Nobody edits anybody else's words.

## Artefacts

- **The map:** every party, what it delivered, who worked with whom. Handed over before anybody is asked for anything.
- **The base story:** written first, at the author's own risk.
- **The shared document:** one document every party writes into. The author and the customer choose where it lives.
- **The channel approval table:** at the end of the shared document. One row per party, filled in by that party only.
- **The NDA and the publication consent:** identical for every party of one Story. Every author brings their own. A [basic NDA template](templates/mutual-nda.md) is in this repository.
- **The channels:** blog (as an Open Ecosystem Success Story), GitHub (in this repository, the canonical version), media (print and online).

## Process

| Phase | What happens | Window |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Foundation | The author gathers the traces, draws the map, writes the base story. Nobody is asked anything yet | As long as it takes |
| 1 · Customer | The customer checks the base story, approves the project and sends one introduction mail to every partner | **Max 2 weeks** |
| 2 · The ask | The author sends every partner the same mail on the same day: map, shared document, the rule, timeline, costs, channels | One day |
| 3 · In or out | Each partner says yes or no. Silence is not a yes | **Max 2 weeks** |
| 4 · Text and quotes | Each party writes its own section and quote | **Min 2 months** |
| 5 · Approval | Every party fills in its row of the approval table and signs the publication consent | 2 weeks |
| 6 · Publication | All channels, same day, every party through its own network | One day |

The customer's introduction says plainly that a no has no effect on the partner's relationship with the customer.

If a medium shortens the text, the short version goes back through the approval table.

If the Story doesn't publish, say so openly, without naming anybody.

## Costs

One publication package, split equally across all parties, the customer included. Payable only on publication. Equal, not proportionate: proportionate money would buy proportionate weight, and that's what this format removes.

## Lessons from the first run

1. **At least two months for text and quotes.** One month wasn't enough to clear legal, marketing and a budget holder.
2. **Maximum two weeks for a no.** A no is free on day one and total on the last day. Say it early: you're not being difficult, you're being cheap to work with.
3. **The customer goes first.** Partners answer their customer, not an author they don't know.
4. **Hand over the map before asking.** An ecosystem that can't see itself can't value what it's asked to join.
5. **Send the ask to a named person with authority.** A joint document that lands at legal as a risk gets a no by default.

## What must not happen

- Nobody is named without consent, not even by implication.
- Nobody who declines is named or described.
- The rule is never bent, not for a party and not for a channel.
- No enemy. Never name or imply a company outside the Story.
- Nobody buys a Story.
